---
name: outline-and-write
description: Resume a book project from existing articles, written expert answers or returned recordings and draft the outline and full manuscript.
---

Load the selected 30 questions, field research and available expert material. Accept previously supplied written answers or transcripts in place of a new recording. When audio is supplied, transcribe it or use the supplied transcript. Preserve the original. Index each answer by D ID and source file plus timestamp or paragraph. Reconcile out-of-order answers and later corrections. Keep unmatched or conflicting statements in a private issue log. If audio cannot be read, request a transcript. Record missing parts without filling them in as expert testimony.

Extract the author's recurring phrases, decision rules, characteristic examples and qualifications with source locations. Outline chapters around decisions in the order a reader faces them. Give each chapter a title, purpose, mapped D IDs, opening situation, supporting answers and reader action. Account for every D ID, marking unanswered items. Use each Money Dialog to set the depth and the outline to set the reading order. Inventory existing articles against D01–D30 by question and decision, even when article numbering differs. Use matched articles as starting manuscript material and verify their claims against the expert answers and research. For uncovered questions, develop the answer directly to comparable standalone article depth. Use about 1,500 words per question as a planning benchmark when the evidence supports it. Thirty such treatments provide about 45,000 words before integration.

For each D ID, record the direct answer, reasoning, distinctive example or evidence, conditions or exceptions, next action, and planned chapter or section. Flag thin evidence and missing expert judgment. Allocate chapter length according to the material. Six questions may need several substantial sections or two chapters. A six-question chapter of about 1,100 words warrants a depth review even if it mentions every question.

Target 35,000–45,000 words of main text for a full first manuscript when supported. Record actual main-text word count, excluding front matter and editorial notes. Explain meaningful departures and identify specific gaps. Never pad with generic advice or invented stories. When asked for a pilot, deliver the whole-book outline with estimated chapter lengths and write the requested sample at its planned full depth.

Weave the developed sections together. Introduce shared concepts once and remove repeated hooks and promotional calls to action. Preserve distinct explanations, examples, exceptions and practical guidance. Then write the introduction, connected chapters and closing action plan. Use expert-supported first person, integrate sourced context with attribution and avoid invented cases, numbers or quotations. Keep a separate passage-to-source map. Send the draft, outline, per-question coverage record, word count, source index, voice samples and open issues directly to the editing skill before human review.
